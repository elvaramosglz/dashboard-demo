STEP 1 — PEER GROUPS BY STRUCTURAL SIZE

Cities are grouped by structural marketplace size, measured as average Trips across the full 21-month period. Acceptance Rate, C/RS, RS/SS, Take Rate and other performance metrics are not used in the grouping because those are outcomes the framework is designed to diagnose. This prevents circular logic: peer groups describe scale, not whether a city is performing well or poorly.

Formulas and decision rules
Average Trips (21m)
Average Tripsᵢ = Σ Monthly Tripsᵢ ÷ Number of Months
Portfolio P33
P33 = 33rd percentile of the eight city-level Average Trips values
Portfolio P67
P67 = 67th percentile of the eight city-level Average Trips values
Peer Group
Large if Average Trips ≥ P67; Medium if P33 ≤ Average Trips < P67; Small if Average Trips < P33
Cities in Group
Count of cities with the same Peer Group classification
CoV Trips (21m)
CoVᵢ = Sample Standard Deviation of Monthly Tripsᵢ ÷ Average Tripsᵢ
Portfolio percentile thresholds

A percentile is a portfolio-level cutoff that indicates the value below which a given share of the city-level observations falls. Python first calculates each city's Average Trips across the 21 available months. It then calculates P33 and P67 from the eight resulting city-level averages. P33 separates Small from Medium cities, while P67 separates Medium from Large cities.

Momentum

STEP 5 — DUAL SCORE

This step identifies the core marketplace dimension that is currently the binding constraint. Five core dimensions compete: Intent, Fulfillment, Supply, Experience, and Economics. Each dual score combines the city's own trajectory with its peer position using confidence-dependent weights. NPI remains supporting context and does not enter the constraint score.

Formulas and decision rules
Confidence-weighted dual score
Normal confidence: 60% Own-History + 40% Peer. Low confidence: 80% Own-History + 20% Peer.
Score clipping
Every blended score is clipped to the interval [0.50, 1.20].
Experience composite
50% Acceptance Individual Score + 50% Cancelation Individual Score.
Preliminary constraint
The lowest score among Intent, Fulfillment, Supply, Experience, and Economics.
Final constraint
If the Experience deterioration guardrail is RED, it overrides the preliminary constraint.
NPI context
NPI does not determine the constraint; it is retained as supporting diagnostic context.

STEP 6 — CLASSIFICATION

Classifies each city by combining the final constraint, own-history health, peer-relative health, Experience guardrails, momentum and recent persistence. Momentum enters at this stage—not in the Dual Score. Any red Experience guardrail overrides the 2×2 classification, so a city with a critical guardrail breach cannot be labeled Healthy.

Own History Status
Healthy when the constraint’s own-history score is ≥ Healthy Score Threshold; otherwise Deteriorated.
Peer Status
Healthy when the constraint’s peer score is ≥ Healthy Score Threshold; otherwise Deteriorated.
Persistence
Counts how many of the three recent months breach the city’s own historical Q1/Q3 reference boundary: 0 Not Persistent, 1 Emerging / Isolated, 2 Repeated, 3 Persistent.
Base Classification
Healthy = own healthy + peer healthy; Deteriorating = own deteriorated + peer healthy; Structural Gap = own healthy + peer deteriorated; Critical Constraint = both deteriorated.
Guardrail Override
Any RED Experience guardrail forces Critical Guardrail Breach regardless of the base 2×2 result.
Trajectory Labels
Momentum below 0.95 is Declining; above 1.05 is Improving; otherwise Stable. Supply adds its operating-band position to the label.

Constraint-specific logic
The Final Constraint determines the mechanism library, operational owner, primary KPI, guardrails and experiment direction.
Supply context
Supply hypotheses branch by Above Band versus Below Band utilization because the operating implications are opposite.
Economics qualifier
NPI is used as a price-position qualifier: premium pricing can make further monetization risky, while discount pricing may indicate controlled headroom.

STEP 9 — SCENARIO SIMULATOR

Estimates mechanical exposure if the diagnosed metric moved partway toward a defensible target while all other factors are held constant. These are scenarios—not causal forecasts. Conservative, Base and Upper Bound close 25%, 50% and 100% of the observed gap by default.

Intent scenario
Incremental Trips = Shopping Sessions × (Target Intent − Current Intent) × Current Fulfillment × Gap-Closure %.
Fulfillment / Supply scenario
Incremental Trips = Requesting Sessions × (Target Fulfillment − Current Fulfillment) × Gap-Closure %.
Economics scenario
Incremental GB = Gross Bookings × (Target Take Rate − Current Take Rate) × Gap-Closure %.
Experience scenario
Uses a mechanical Trips-per-Request proxy only; it is an illustrative upper bound, not a validated causal relationship.
